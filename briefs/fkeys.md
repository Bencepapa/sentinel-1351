# Task: what do the function keys F1-F7 do in the C64 original? (read-only investigation, issue #23)

User knows: F1 = PAUSE on the C64. Unknown what F3, F5 do (F7 = restart?). Trace the keyboard handling in the JS
C64 machine (KEYMAP $FDEC, the key scan in the flight/map/fight loops; .zen/lib.js helpers, js/src/hooks.js,
SentinelDisasm, states in briefs/ref/*.state.json; press keys through m.setKey with the C64 matrix). For every
function key (with and without shift) find: address of the handler, conditions (in which mode it works: flight,
fight, map, base sequence, end screen), effect, messages/sounds shown, state changed (difficulty? restart?
pause?). Also check other special keys the game reads (RESTORE, RUN/STOP, space, C=, the letter keys such as
A/T). Append the result to this file below a line "## Findings": a table key -> effect -> handler -> modes, plus
how pause behaves (does it freeze the clock, sound, music? is there a PAUSED text? how it is left) and how
restart (probably F7) re-initialises (which routine; is the difficulty menu involved), plus a proposal how to
port each to the C128 port (the C128 keyboard matrix has its own F-key positions; list what the port's input
code in c128-game already reads) with byte estimates. Do not change code. No commit/push. End with a short
summary.

## Findings

In the C64 game, **F1 pauses, F3 skips the rest of the friendly-base repair report, F5 opens the difficulty menu from the title, and F7 starts from the title or restarts an active game**. The four shifted labels have the same actions as their unshifted partners.

### Function keys

| Key | Effect | C64 handler | Modes and conditions |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 / F2 (Shift+F1) | Latches pause, mutes SID and holds the HUD time colon steady. Joystick directions or fire resume. | `$FDE1` → raster IRQ `$0B69–$0B9B`; main-loop wait `$99F3` | Flight, fighter approach, combat and auto-tracking, Stellar Grid, warp, mothership mines/rings and friendly repair. It also pauses the opening death flash and the victory wait. The difficulty screen can latch it too. Title/attract mode bypasses pause while `$88` is set. The death report runs with IRQs disabled and ignores it. |
| F3 / F4 (Shift+F3) | Advances past the eight-system inspection animation. The recharge still completes, the ship is repaired and it receives one proton. Skips any remaining report-row updates and their beeps. | `$FDE2` → `$9403`, then `$940B–$9433` and `SHIPINIT $8451` | Friendly docking only, once the text has been drawn and the report passes through `$93D6`. Holding it through the drawing phase works when the check is reached. No effect in normal flight, fights, map, mines, enemy base phases, difficulty, death or victory. |
| F5 / F6 (Shift+F5) | Opens the five-choice difficulty screen. Vertical joystick changes the highlight/level, and fire confirms it. The normal Starfleet message follows. | `$FDE3` → `$85FE/$8603` → `$8609`; menu loop `$864A`, selection store `$87C6`, confirm `$867F` | Title/attract only. Once the menu is up, F5 neither changes the level nor confirms it. No in-game difficulty hotkey. |
| F7 / F8 (Shift+F7) | On the title, starts the same difficulty screen as F5 or fire. In play, abandons the mission and returns to the title. On the death/victory report, restarts after the corresponding input gate. | Title `$2DC6–$2DF9` → `$8609`; active play and victory `$99F3–$9A01` → `$245A`; death `$899D–$89BD` → `$245A` | Active flight, approach/tracking, combat, open map, warp, mines/rings and repair poll through `$99F3`. The difficulty menu has its own loop and uses fire. During pause, `$99F3` waits for resume before it can read F7. The death path polls F7 directly after the report delay. The victory path calls `$99F3` during its wait, so F7 bypasses the **fire** lockout there. |

The common scan `KEYSCAN1 $FDEE` strobes CIA1 PA0 with `$FE` and compares the **whole** CIA1 PB byte at `$FE06`. F1, F3, F5 and F7 read `$EF/$DF/$BF/$F7` and set `$FDE1/$FDE2/$FDE3/$FDE4` respectively. The raster service at `$0B43` schedules it every fourth update. The Shift keys sit on PA1/PB7 and PA6/PB4, so either one can be held without altering a single function key's PB sample. Two keys on PA0 together produce a different PB byte and fail that single-key comparison. `KEYSCAN2 $FE36` handles the `$FDEC` C= flag and SPACE, and `KEYSCAN3 $FE6E` handles W and A. The 1351 patch substitutes its presence check at `$0B43` and changes the scan's idle PA value from `$7F` to `$BF` for the port-2 pots. The function-key comparisons and dispatch branches are the same in the extracted joystick original.

The main-loop check at `$99F3` is called at `$2CF1` in the flight/fight loop, `$C6B2` on approach, `$8B20` in auto-tracking, `$B53A/$B6A8/$B72C` during warp, and `$8C9A/$9066/$9122/$93F0` through the base sequence. An open Stellar Grid runs inside those flight or approach loops. Entering difficulty through either title key calls `$18B4` to clear attract mode and gate off its three SID voices. The difficulty screen has no separate key cue.

### Pause and restart

- **Pause.** `$0B8A` sets `$9F=1`, consumes `$FDE1`, writes `$00` to SID mode/volume `$D418`, sets the border-colour operand `$0CCC` to light blue `$0E`, and forces the time colon at `$544C` to its visible glyph `$6E`. There is no PAUSED message or confirmation sound. IRQs keep the raster splits and keyboard scan alive. The pause guards skip normal IRQ work, including the `$0BF6` call to the HUD tick `$FAD7`, and `$99F3` holds the main thread at its next check, stopping the world tick `$BE29`, gameplay and SID sequencing. The HUD tick's 60-count divider `$FABD` is preserved. The tunnel's phase advance also checks `$9F` at `$0E21`. The mute applies to music and effects through SID master volume. There is no music or effect cue on unpause.
- **Resume.** `$0B76` reads raw joystick port 2 via `$DC00 & $1F`; any direction or fire restores `$D418=$1F` and clears `$9F`. F1 again, releasing F1, and 1351 pointer motion alone leave the game paused. The 1351 buttons share port-2 lines and can resume. A held F1 can keep asserting pause on subsequent scans. The menu's first pause can leave its foreground selection loop running against the last sampled input, while input refresh and the IRQ clock remain stopped.
- **Restart.** All in-game F7 branches converge on `GAMEENTRY $245A`. It disables/acknowledges the raster IRQ, resets the stack with `TXS`, blanks the VIC, clears `$D400–$D418`, and calls the state reset at `$F6C1`. That clears pause, map/warp/combat flags and mission counters and resets difficulty `$E0` to 0 (Skirmish). Startup regenerates the Stellar Grid with `$20F1`, restores eight protons and initializes shields/systems through `$8451`, and calls `IRQINIT $0A80` for the raster setup, HUD clock reset `$FABE` and full energy `$F86C`. It returns to the normal title/attract loop and its sound. The difficulty menu is a separate entry at `$8609`, reachable again from the title. The key is level-polled, so a long hold can take the new title into the menu. There is no extra restart message or confirmation sound.
- **End screens.** The death report's `$899D` invokes the keyboard scan directly once its delay expires, even though the raster IRQ is off. F7 and raw fire share the reset branch, and the timer can also take it. Victory's `$F60D–$F631` has a raw fire gate and a timeout plus the normal F7/pause check at `$F62C`. Pausing there stops the report wait loop too. This is separate from the C64 restart/arrival raster-compare bug documented in `js/src/hooks.js`.

At the opening death flash (`$8882–$88CA`), F1 suppresses IRQ animation at `$8834`, so the transition to the report stalls until resume while the foreground loop keeps spinning. F7 waits for the report's input gate. Brief base transitions have local loops. F1 can latch in the IRQ, and the foreground stops at a pause check or when it waits for an IRQ-driven effect. F7 is handled at the next common check, for example after the tunnel fade at `$8D2F` or the post-hit effect at `$8F53`.

### Other inputs

| Key / control | C64 effect and handler |
|---|---|
| RESTORE | A hardware NMI. The RAM vector `$FFFA/$FFFB` points to `$0AB9`, which switches banks, copies `$0600–$07FF` to `$6000–$61FF` and jumps to `$245A`. It is an immediate restart to title, including when the main loop is paused. RUN/STOP is unnecessary and does not change this into a BASIC exit. |
| RUN/STOP (PA7/PB7) | No game action. `$FE36` does not match its PB pattern. |
| SPACE (PA7/PB4) | `$FDE5`. In normal flight/combat `$2E1F`, and during auto-tracking `$8AE5`, arm the proton path `$BACE/$BADA` when allowed. Visible base approach checks it at `$9136`, where hit timing and the base opening matter. The mine/settling loops have no separate SPACE launch. It also accepts the Starfleet briefing (`$9999`) and can cancel the Stellar Grid when the cursor is on the current non-base sector (`$FBDB/$FBFB`). A different sector requires fire at `$FC62`. Launch effects use the normal proton sound and stock counter. |
| C= (PA7/PB5) | `KEYMAP $FDEC`, read at `$FB5A` inside `$FB43`. Opens the Stellar Grid from flight, approach or combat/tracking when the attract/proton guards allow it. The grid has its usual beeps. C= alone does not close it, and the base loops do not call this handler. The 1351 C64 patch also maps a held right mouse button to the grid. |
| A (PA1/PB2) | `$FDE7` → new-press check `$8C07`. In the flight/fight loop `$2D7A` requests auto-tracking via `$62`; `$89E6` checks the tracking computer at `$3D` and the active tracking loop `$8A34` uses the next press to disengage. Engagement sets the tracking state and draws **AUTO TRACKING ENGAGED** (string at `$184B`, display call at `$8A2C`), using the tracking crosshair. This is meaningful with a trackable group and working scanner/computer. |
| T (PA2/PB6) | No C64 binding. This column is absent from the game's three scan routines. T is a C128 port alias for A. |
| W (PA1/PB1) | Hold for manual thrust in flight and combat/tracking (`$2D0E/$8B1D` → `THRUST $FEA7`, blocked by `$BA` during transit). Sets `$63/$FEA6`, spends energy at `$F8B2`, ramps the thrust/depth value `$F209` towards 5 and updates the voice-3 frequency high byte at `$D40F`. Release ramps back towards `$41`. The tracking loop exits on manual thrust. |
| CRSR left/right (PA0/PB2), either Shift state | `$FDED` → `$B442` during acceleration or `$B5DD` in the jump. Early abort shortens acceleration, skips cruise and sends the ship back towards its current sector. Late abort shortens cruise and substitutes random destination coordinates before deceleration. The normal warp presentation handles the transition. The other legacy cursor key (PA0/PB7) has no abort binding. |
| 1, 2, Z, S | The scanners sample these into `$FDEA/$FDEB/$FDE8/$FDE9`. There are no gameplay consumers of those four flags. |

### C128 port proposal

The native C128's **four F-key positions are the same CIA1 positions** as the C64's. Strobe PA0 with `$FE`, and read PB4, PB5, PB6 and PB3 for F1, F3, F5 and F7. The even labels use Shift on the same physical switches. The three `$D02F` strobes are for the extra columns, including the separate top cursor cluster. The legacy CRSR left/right switch is PA0/PB2. The dedicated top left/right keys are K2/PB5 and K2/PB6 if support for those is desired. [Commodore 128 Programmer's Reference Guide, keyboard table](https://www.pagetable.com/docs/Commodore%20128%20Programmer%27s%20Reference%20Guide.pdf) and [VICE C128 keyboard matrix](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/VICE-Team/svn-mirror/main/vice/data/C128/gtk3_sym.vkm).

The joined build already reads joystick port 2 and 1351 pots/buttons in cockpit `INPUT` and `MAPIN`. `TKEYS` reads A and T, and `HINPUT` uses H (PA3/PB5) to toggle the hyperspace cursor test. The frame tail patched in `c128-game/build.py` strobes PA7 for SPACE → `GSPACE` and C= → `MAPKEY/GMAPTOG`, including the held right-button alias. C= currently toggles the grid, and the port already has its own launch/phase guards. The same tail reads the RUN/STOP bit with no action. `GAMEINIT` vectors bank-0 RESTORE to `NMIRTI`. Ending input `ENDTICK` checks raw fire after its lockout and also times out. The port boots straight into play with shared `DIFF=0`. It has no title or difficulty screen. See `c128-gui/cockpit/cockpit.asm`, `c128-game/game.asm`, `c128-game/build.py` and `c128-game/ending.asm`.

Keep the hot poll behind a fixed-size hook, put the reset dispatcher in resident RAM, and check the result with JS DMA and x128 for split timing, `OVRUN`, pause/resume and resets while MINES or ENDING owns the overlay.

### Verification

A staged three-fighter encounter reached the active tracking loop at `$8A34`. F1 held both counters and all three active fighter slots steady, port-2 left resumed play, A reached the tracking exit at `$8A4E`, and F7 reached `$245A`.

Disassembled the loaded C64 image with `.zen/lib.js` and `SentinelDisasm`, compared its input paths with `prg/original/SN1.prg` and `SN2.prg`, and traced the actual 6510 branches with `js/src/hooks.js`. In the JS machine, used `m.setKey` for single presses and Shift combinations, `joy2` and 1351 input to test resume, and a CPU NMI for RESTORE. Tests used a cold title, booted flight, the supplied `c64-last-enemy-map-then-win`, `c64-minefield` and `c64-proton-in-flight-mothership` snapshots, and reached or staged the report, menu and ending waits. The browser's existing raster-compare fix was active in the runtime tests so the restart could be followed back to the title. Neither the key dispatch nor pause was patched by that hook.

Over a 90-frame pause in flight, open map, mines, rings and victory, the HUD divider and world tick counter held steady while raster IRQs continued and the SID write log stayed empty after the mute write. F1 again and mouse motion left the state paused. Raw direction, raw fire and both 1351 buttons resumed it. The staged friendly report left its inspection loop after 3 frames with F3 and 150 frames with no key. Both runs restored the damaged system and raised proton stock from 5 to 6. CRSR left/right returned from acceleration to the departure sector, 05.03, and retargeted to 03.02 in the sampled cruise run. CRSR up/down did neither. Both Shift switches were exercised across the matrix probes and targeted cases. The C128 proposal was checked against source and the hardware matrix, without implementing it or measuring assembled size on x128.
